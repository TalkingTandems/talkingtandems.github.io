Scenery and points of interest

Starting and finishing at Scone Airport, this 97 km circular ride takes cyclists on an extensive journey through the countryside of Perthshire and Angus. The outward route heads towards Pitroddie and Kilspindie before continuing through Kinnaird and Abernyte. From here, the ride travels northwards towards Newtyle and Meigle, linking a succession of rural communities and providing a changing sense of place as the journey progresses.

Beyond Meigle, the route continues towards Alyth before reaching Bridgend of Lintrathen and Airlie. This northern section of the circuit takes riders into the countryside around the Angus villages, forming the furthest part of the journey before the route turns southwards again. The combination of smaller settlements and longer stretches between them gives this ride a distinctly rural character.

The return journey passes through Meigle for a second time before continuing towards Ardler, Collace, Kinrossie and Balbeggie. From Balbeggie, the route heads back towards Scone Airport, completing a substantial circuit through two neighbouring regions. With almost 100 km of cycling, there is plenty of opportunity for pilots and stokers to enjoy the shared experience of travelling through this part of Scotland.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

At 97 km (approximately 60 miles), this is a substantial circular ride, classified as moderately difficult. Although the overall climbing appears relatively manageable for a route of this length, the distance alone makes it a demanding day out for many tandem crews. A comfortable pace, good communication and planned breaks will help make the ride enjoyable for both pilot and stoker.

  • Distance: Approximately 97 km (60 miles), starting and finishing at Scone Airport. This is a full-day outing for many groups, particularly when allowing for refreshments and rest stops.
  • Climbing: Available route estimates suggest approximately 523 metres of total ascent. The climbing is spread across the circuit, with some shorter uphill sections that may require a change of pace and gearing.
  • Descending: Around 523 metres of descent is also estimated. Tandem pilots should anticipate changes in gradient and allow adequate time and distance for controlled braking.
  • Route character: The ride links numerous villages and rural communities across Perthshire and Angus, including Abernyte, Newtyle, Meigle, Alyth and Collace. The precise road types and any path sections should be checked before the ride.
  • Effort and pacing: Despite its moderately difficult classification, the near-100 km distance calls for reasonable endurance. Crews should consider their experience of longer rides and aim for a sustainable pace throughout.
  • Tandem considerations: Clear communication about upcoming climbs, descents, junctions and stops will help pilots and stokers work together. Regular opportunities to stretch, eat and drink are especially valuable on a ride of this length.
