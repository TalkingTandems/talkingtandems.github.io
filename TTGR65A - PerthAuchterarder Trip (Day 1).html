Scenery and points of interest

The Auchterarder Trip (Day 1) is the first stage of a two-day Talking Tandems adventure, taking riders on a 35.9-mile journey from Dalgety Bay Sailing Club towards Perth. Leaving Dalgety Bay, the route heads north through Fife, passing Lumphinnans and Keltybridge before continuing towards Milnathort.

Beyond Milnathort, the ride continues through the countryside towards Duncrievie, with a mixture of open rural stretches and smaller villages along the way. The changing landscape provides plenty of variety as the route makes its way north towards Perth.

The final part of the journey approaches Perth before heading towards Ruthvenfield and the planned overnight accommodation at Broxden Travelodge. After a full day's cycling, there's an opportunity to relax and enjoy an evening together before setting off on the second day's ride.

  • Dalgety Bay Sailing Club: The familiar starting point for the first day of the trip.
  • Lumphinnans and Keltybridge: Two of the places passed through as the route heads north across Fife.
  • Milnathort: A useful landmark as the ride progresses towards Perthshire.
  • Duncrievie: A rural section of the journey between Milnathort and Perth.
  • Perth and Ruthvenfield: The final part of the day's route before reaching the overnight accommodation.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

This 35.9-mile ride is rated Moderately Difficult, offering a satisfying first day of the two-day Auchterarder trip. Although the overall climbing is manageable for experienced tandem cyclists, the distance and changing terrain make it a ride worth pacing comfortably.

  • Distance: Approximately 35.9 miles (57.8 km), making this a reasonably substantial day's cycling.
  • Climbing: An estimated 433 metres of ascent, with several undulating sections and some shorter climbs requiring additional effort.
  • Descents: Approximately 370 metres of descent, providing opportunities to recover between climbs. Tandem pilots should maintain controlled speeds, particularly on unfamiliar downhill sections.
  • Gradient: Some short uphill stretches may approach 8%, requiring suitable gearing and coordinated effort between pilot and stoker.
  • Route characteristics: The journey passes through towns, villages and rural areas. Road surfaces, junctions and any path sections should be checked before the group ride.
  • Tandem considerations: Allow time for comfortable climbing, regular breaks and clear communication between pilot and stoker. The distance makes steady pacing particularly important, especially with another day's cycling to follow.
