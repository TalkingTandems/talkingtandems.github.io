Scenery and points of interest

The Hangover Ride is a short, friendly outing through the south of Fife, starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. At just under 13 miles, it's an opportunity to enjoy some fresh air, good company and a relaxed tandem ride without committing to a full day in the saddle.

Leaving Dalgety Bay, the route heads towards Inverkeithing before continuing south to North Queensferry, close to the Firth of Forth. This part of Fife brings together established communities, coastal surroundings and the distinctive setting of North Queensferry beneath the Forth bridges. The journey offers pilots and stokers plenty to talk about as they make their way between the towns.

From North Queensferry, the route returns through Inverkeithing and onwards to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. With its relatively short distance and familiar starting point, the Hangover Ride is a sociable way to get the legs turning and enjoy cycling together.

  • Dalgety Bay Sailing Club: The starting and finishing point for the ride.
  • Inverkeithing: A familiar Fife town visited on both the outward and return journeys.
  • North Queensferry: The southernmost destination of the ride, beside the Firth of Forth.
  • The Forth bridges area: An impressive geographical setting around North Queensferry, although views from the precise route have not been confirmed.
  • A circular outing: Return to Dalgety Bay by way of Inverkeithing, completing a manageable 12.9-mile ride.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

The Hangover Ride is graded Easy, making it a good choice for a shorter, sociable tandem outing. Although the distance is modest, the route is not completely flat, so pilots and stokers should expect a little climbing as they travel between Dalgety Bay, Inverkeithing and North Queensferry.

  • Distance: Approximately 12.9 miles, starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club.
  • Climbing: The route includes several changes in elevation, with some short uphill sections. The available route profile suggests around 148 metres of total climbing.
  • Descents: Expect corresponding downhill stretches, particularly around the approach to and return from North Queensferry. Tandem pilots should maintain a comfortable, controlled speed.
  • Effort: Generally an easy ride, although the short climbs may require a little extra effort, especially when restarting or maintaining momentum on a tandem.
  • Route character: The ride travels from Dalgety Bay through Inverkeithing to North Queensferry before returning by the same general sequence of places. Specific road and path conditions have not been confirmed.
  • Tandem teamwork: Good communication between pilot and stoker will help with approaching climbs, preparing for descents, changing pace and making the most of a relaxed group ride.
