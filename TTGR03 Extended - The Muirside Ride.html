Scenery and points of interest

Starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club, this 25.8-mile circular ride takes Talking Tandems on a varied journey through western Fife. The route begins through Hillend and Inverkeithing before heading inland through Duloch towards Townhill and Bowershall, offering a change from the coastal settlements to the higher ground around Dunfermline.

From Bowershall, the ride continues through Muirside and Craigluscar before turning towards Crossford. This western section takes the group through a succession of smaller Fife communities, with the route gradually returning towards lower ground as it heads south.

The final part of the journey passes along Waggon Road towards Charleston and Limekilns, bringing the ride back towards the Firth of Forth. From Limekilns, the route continues through Rosyth and Ferry Toll before returning through Inverkeithing to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. It's a satisfying circuit that combines inland Fife with the coastal character of the villages along the Forth.

  • Townhill and Bowershall: The route's progression towards higher ground north of Dunfermline.
  • Muirside and Craigluscar: Smaller communities marking the western part of the circuit.
  • Crossford: A useful landmark as the route turns south towards the Forth.
  • Charleston and Limekilns: Two coastal villages that provide a change of setting towards the end of the ride.
  • Rosyth and Ferry Toll: The final stages before returning through Inverkeithing to Dalgety Bay.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

This moderately difficult circular ride covers approximately 25.8 miles, with a mixture of climbing, descending and more gently changing terrain. It offers a worthwhile outing for Talking Tandems, with enough variation to make teamwork and sensible pacing important.

  • Distance: Approximately 25.8 miles, starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club.
  • Climbing: The route gains height as it heads inland through Duloch towards Townhill, Bowershall and Craigluscar. Allow time for a comfortable climbing pace.
  • Higher ground: The route reaches approximately 500 feet above sea level around its inland section, so expect a noticeable change in elevation from the start.
  • Descending: The return towards Crossford, Charleston and Limekilns includes an overall loss of height. Tandem pilots should manage speed carefully and communicate upcoming changes in gradient.
  • Effort: The moderately difficult classification makes this a ride best approached with a steady, sustainable pace, particularly on the inland climbs.
  • Tandem teamwork: Clear communication about climbs, descents, junctions and changes of direction will help pilots and stokers enjoy the route together. Allow suitable opportunities for regrouping and refreshments.
