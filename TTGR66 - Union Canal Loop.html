Scenery and points of interest

Starting from Dalgety Bay Sailing Club, this enjoyable 27.3-mile circular ride takes Talking Tandems across the Firth of Forth and into the countryside of West Lothian. Heading through Inverkeithing, the route crosses the Forth Road Bridge to South Queensferry, where the three great Forth bridges form one of Scotland's most recognisable landmarks. The crossing provides a memorable beginning to the journey, with the Forth and its impressive engineering heritage giving this ride a distinctive character.

From South Queensferry, the route heads inland through Kirkliston towards Winchburgh, before continuing through Faucheldean, Kingscavil, Philipstoun and Old Philipstoun. This section explores the villages and countryside around the Union Canal corridor, exchanging the dramatic setting of the Forth bridges for a more rural landscape. The journey then turns east through Threemiletown and Abercorn, linking a series of smaller communities as the tandems make their way back towards the Forth.

The return passes through the Hopetoun area and Port Edgar before reaching South Queensferry once again. Crossing the Forth Road Bridge for a second time brings the riders back into Fife, with the final stretch through Inverkeithing returning everyone to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. Combining two memorable bridge crossings, West Lothian villages and the countryside around the Union Canal, this is a varied and satisfying outing for pilots and stokers alike.

Route highlights
  • Forth Road Bridge: Two crossings of this iconic bridge, providing a memorable journey between Fife and West Lothian.
  • South Queensferry: A historic town on the southern shore of the Forth, closely associated with the three Forth bridges.
  • Kirkliston and Winchburgh: Communities marking the transition from the Forth towards the West Lothian countryside.
  • Union Canal corridor: The route explores the countryside around Winchburgh and Philipstoun, an area associated with the historic Union Canal.
  • Abercorn and Hopetoun: A return through the rural landscape and historic estate surroundings west of South Queensferry.
  • Port Edgar: A waterfront location near the southern end of the Forth Road Bridge, marking the approach to the return crossing.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

Difficulty: Moderately Difficult

The Union Canal Loop is a varied 27.3-mile circular ride combining two crossings of the Forth Road Bridge with the gently changing countryside of West Lothian. Although the overall climbing is relatively modest, the distance, bridge approaches and numerous changes of direction make this an enjoyable challenge for tandem teams comfortable with a half-day outing.

  • Distance: Approximately 27.3 miles (43.9 km), starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. Allow time for regrouping, refreshments and enjoying the different stages of the ride.
  • Climbing: Approximately 172 metres of cumulative ascent are estimated. The route rises from the low-lying Forth coast towards the inland countryside around Winchburgh and Kingscavil, with smaller changes in elevation throughout.
  • Descending: Around 172 metres of cumulative descent are estimated. The return towards Abercorn, Hopetoun and Port Edgar generally brings the route back towards lower elevations near the Forth.
  • Gradients: Elevations range from approximately 6 to 98 metres above sea level. No particularly steep sustained climb has been identified, although shorter rises may still require a timely gear change and coordinated effort.
  • Route character: The circuit includes two crossings of the Forth Road Bridge, followed by a journey through Kirkliston, Winchburgh, Faucheldean, Kingscavil, Philipstoun, Threemiletown and Abercorn. Pilots should anticipate junctions, changes of direction and the need for careful navigation through the various communities.
  • Tandem considerations: The bridge crossings deserve particular attention, especially when allowing space for the tandem and maintaining a controlled pace. Wind exposure may affect handling on the bridge, so pilots and stokers should communicate clearly and be prepared to adjust their speed. A steady cadence and sensible pacing will help make the inland sections comfortable for both riders.

Overall: A rewarding, moderately difficult ride combining the excitement of crossing the Forth with a varied tour of West Lothian's villages and countryside. A particularly enjoyable choice for tandem teams looking for something different from their usual Fife circuits.
