Scenery and points of interest

Setting out from Perth Travelodge, this 54.5-mile ride takes Talking Tandems on a rewarding journey south through Perth and Kinross and into Fife, finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. It is a substantial day in the saddle, combining a succession of towns and villages with the changing landscapes of central Scotland.

The route begins around Ruthvenfield before heading through Balgowan and St Davids towards Gleneagles. From here, the journey continues south towards Powmill, passing through an area of rolling countryside where the changing elevations provide variety and a satisfying sense of progress. With the longer distance comes plenty of opportunity for pilots and stokers to settle into a comfortable rhythm and enjoy the journey together.

The final stages take the tandems through Saline, Wellwood and Crossford, before passing through Inverkeithing and returning to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. The route offers an enjoyable transition from the countryside of Perth and Kinross to the familiar communities of western Fife, providing a fitting conclusion to a full day's cycling.

  • Ruthvenfield and Balgowan: The opening section of the journey, heading away from Perth and into the countryside.
  • Gleneagles: A distinctive geographical milestone as the ride makes its way south through Perth and Kinross.
  • Powmill: A useful reference point as the route enters the western Fife area.
  • Saline and Wellwood: Two more communities marking the progression towards the final stages.
  • Crossford and Inverkeithing: Familiar western Fife locations on the approach to Dalgety Bay.
  • Dalgety Bay Sailing Club: The finishing point and a welcome end to the day's ride.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

Classified as Moderately Difficult, this 54.5-mile ride offers a satisfying endurance challenge for Talking Tandems pilots and stokers. Although the overall climbing appears relatively manageable for the distance, the length of the journey and its undulating sections call for steady pacing, good teamwork and sensible breaks.

  • Distance: Approximately 54.5 miles (87.7 km), making this a longer outing that will require a reasonable level of cycling fitness and stamina.
  • Climbing: Approximately 408 metres of cumulative ascent are estimated, with the higher sections occurring during the journey through Perth and Kinross. Expect a mixture of gentle rises and more noticeable climbs.
  • Descending: Around 403 metres of cumulative descent are estimated. Pilots should manage speed carefully, particularly where gradients, bends or junctions require additional attention.
  • Elevation and gradients: The route reaches approximately 268 metres above sea level. No particularly steep, sustained climb has been identified, although shorter uphill sections may still require lower gears and coordinated effort.
  • Route character: The journey passes through a mixture of settlements and countryside, including Gleneagles, Powmill, Saline and Crossford. Pilots should be prepared for changing road conditions and take particular care when navigating junctions and unfamiliar sections.
  • Tandem considerations: A steady cadence, early gear changes and clear communication between pilot and stoker will help keep the ride comfortable. Allow time for rest, refreshments and regrouping, particularly as fatigue develops towards the final miles.

Overall: A varied, longer-distance ride best enjoyed at a comfortable, sustainable pace, with teamwork and shared achievement at the heart of the experience.
