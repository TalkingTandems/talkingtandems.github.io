Scenery and points of interest

Starting in Strathmiglo, this delightful 27.9-mile circular ride explores a varied corner of Fife and the neighbouring Perthshire countryside. The route begins with a climb towards Glen Tarkie before continuing through Abernethy Glen, where the surrounding hills and rural landscape provide a distinctly different atmosphere from the villages further along the journey. From here, the ride descends towards Abernethy, a historic settlement at the foot of the Ochil Hills, before continuing east to Newburgh, close to the southern shore of the River Tay.

Leaving Newburgh, the route turns inland through the Den of Lindores, exchanging the lower-lying countryside near the Tay for the undulating landscape of north Fife. The journey continues through Auchtermuchty and Dunshalt before reaching Freuchie and Newton of Falkland. This section brings together a succession of traditional Fife villages, giving pilots and stokers a varied journey through the area's rural communities.

The final stretch passes through Falkland, an attractive historic village beneath the Lomond Hills, before returning to Strathmiglo. With its combination of glens, countryside, historic settlements and changing elevations, the Abernethy Loop offers plenty of variety in a relatively compact ride. It is a rewarding outing for tandem teams who enjoy exploring different landscapes together.

  • Newburgh: A town near the southern shore of the River Tay, marking the northern part of the circuit.
  • Den of Lindores: A countryside section on the return towards the villages of north Fife.
  • Falkland: A historic village beneath the Lomond Hills, providing a memorable final landmark before returning to Strathmiglo.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

Difficulty: Moderately Difficult

The Abernethy Loop is a varied 27.9-mile circular ride with a mixture of climbing, descending and gentler stretches through the countryside of north Fife and Perthshire. Although the overall distance is relatively modest, the early hills and occasional steeper gradients make this a satisfying outing for tandem teams comfortable with some climbing.

  • Distance: Approximately 27.9 miles (44.9 km), starting and finishing in Strathmiglo. Allow time for regrouping, refreshments and enjoying the different stages of the journey.
  • Climbing: Approximately 353 metres of cumulative ascent are estimated. One of the more significant climbing sections comes early in the ride towards Glen Tarkie, with further rises encountered on the return through the countryside.
  • Descending: Around 353 metres of cumulative descent are estimated. The route drops from the higher ground towards Abernethy and the lower countryside near Newburgh, so pilots should manage speed carefully and anticipate changes in gradient.
  • Gradients: Elevations range from approximately 10 to 245 metres above sea level. Some short uphill sections may approach 10%, requiring suitable gearing, a steady cadence and coordinated effort from both riders.
  • Route character: The circuit links Strathmiglo, Glen Tarkie, Abernethy Glen, Abernethy, Newburgh, Den of Lindores, Auchtermuchty, Dunshalt, Freuchie, Newton of Falkland and Falkland. Expect a mixture of rural stretches and village approaches, with navigation and junction awareness important throughout.
  • Tandem considerations: The early climbing makes it sensible to settle into a comfortable rhythm from the start. Pilots should select lower gears before steeper rises and communicate upcoming descents or changes in direction. Stokers can help maintain a smooth pedalling rhythm, particularly on the more demanding sections.

Overall: A rewarding, moderately difficult ride combining a challenging start with varied countryside and a succession of attractive Fife villages. A good choice for tandem teams looking for an enjoyable half-day circuit with some worthwhile climbing.
