Scenery and points of interest

Starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club, this 67.4 km circular ride takes tandem crews on an extended journey through the towns, villages and countryside of central Fife. Heading out through Hillend and Inverkeithing, the route continues towards Crossgates and Hill of Beath before reaching Kelty. The changing surroundings provide plenty of variety, from familiar Fife communities to the more open countryside encountered between settlements.

From Kelty, the ride heads east through Lumphinnans, Lochgelly, Cardenden and Auchterderran before continuing towards Findatie and Lochran. This northern section adds an interesting dimension to the journey, taking riders beyond the main towns and through a succession of smaller communities and rural locations. The route then turns south, returning through Kelty and Cowdenbeath before continuing towards Mossmorran.

The final stretch takes riders through Bernard's Smithy and Aberdour before returning to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. The approach through Aberdour brings the ride back towards the Firth of Forth coastline, providing a pleasing contrast with the inland sections. With its combination of longer stretches between settlements, changing landscapes and familiar local landmarks, this is a rewarding day out for tandem crews who enjoy exploring Fife together.

Route Highlights

Crossgates and Hill of Beath: The route heads inland through these established Fife communities on its way towards Kelty.

Kelty and Lumphinnans: A transition into the central Fife section of the ride, with Kelty visited twice during the loop.

Lochgelly, Cardenden and Auchterderran: A succession of towns and villages forming the eastern section of the journey.

Findatie and Lochran: The northern part of the loop, adding variety through smaller settlements and countryside locations.

Cowdenbeath and Mossmorran: Familiar central Fife landmarks on the return journey.

Bernard's Smithy and Aberdour: The final section brings riders back towards the Forth coastline before returning to Dalgety Bay.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

At 67.4 km (approximately 42 miles), this is a substantial Talking Tandems outing, offering a varied ride through central Fife. Although classified as moderately difficult, the distance makes it a ride best suited to tandem crews comfortable spending several hours in the saddle. A steady pace, good communication and well-planned breaks will help everyone enjoy the journey.

What to Expect

Distance and endurance: Covering approximately 42 miles, this is a longer club ride. Allow sufficient time for the full circuit, including navigation, rest stops and any unexpected delays.

Climbing: The route rises from Dalgety Bay towards the higher ground around Hill of Beath and Kelty. The available route profile suggests approximately 320 m of cumulative climbing, spread across the ride rather than concentrated into one confirmed major ascent.

Descents: The route includes downhill sections as well as climbs, particularly as it returns towards the lower ground around Aberdour and Dalgety Bay. Pilots should maintain a comfortable, controlled speed and communicate changes in pace to their stoker.

Route character: The journey passes through numerous towns and villages, including Inverkeithing, Lochgelly, Cardenden, Kelty and Cowdenbeath. Crews should be prepared for frequent navigation decisions and changes in direction, particularly through built-up areas.

Tandem teamwork: Longer distances and repeated changes in gradient make smooth gear changes, coordinated pedalling and clear communication particularly important. Pilots should give early notice of approaching climbs, descents, junctions and planned stops.

Pacing and refreshments: With no designated lunch stop, crews should plan suitable opportunities for refreshments and rest. The length of the route makes regular breaks worthwhile, especially before the final return through Aberdour to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club.
