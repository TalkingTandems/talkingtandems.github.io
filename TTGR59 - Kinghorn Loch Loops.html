Scenery and points of interest

Starting from Dalgety Bay Sailing Club, this challenging circular ride explores the varied landscape of southern Fife, combining the coastal towns of Aberdour and Burntisland with the inland roads and hills around Kinghorn Loch. Heading through Aberdour, Whitehill X-roads and Bernard's Smithy, the route tackles its first climb of Binn Hill before descending eastwards along Jawbanes Road. Continuing through Manorleys X-roads and down Redpath Brae, the ride reaches Kinghorn Loch, providing a welcome change of pace after the earlier climbing.

Kinghorn Loch offers an attractive setting for a well-earned lunch break, with The Nest Café at the Fife Ecology Centre a suggested stopping point. Alternatively, riders can continue into Burntisland, where other cafés provide refreshment options. From Burntisland, the route turns inland once more, returning over Binn Hill in the opposite direction. This second crossing brings another demanding climb and descent, giving the ride its distinctive double-loop character.

The homeward journey continues through Bernard's Smithy and over Cullaloe Pass before heading towards Mossmorran, Mossgreen and Crossgates. From there, the route follows North Road through Inverkeithing and Hillend, eventually returning to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. With its combination of lochside surroundings, coastal settlements and repeated hill climbs, Kinghorn Loch Loops offers a rewarding day out for experienced tandem crews.

Route highlights

  • Aberdour: An early coastal village on the outward journey.
  • Binn Hill: Two crossings in opposite directions, each offering a different climbing and descending experience.
  • Jawbanes Road and Redpath Brae: Two named descents on the approach towards Kinghorn Loch.
  • Kinghorn Loch: A natural focal point of the ride and a suggested location for lunch at The Nest Café.
  • Burntisland: A coastal town offering alternative refreshment opportunities before the return climbs.
  • Cullaloe Pass: A further ascent on the homeward leg, adding to the day's climbing challenge.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

Kinghorn Loch Loops is a very difficult 27.3-mile ride, best suited to experienced tandem crews comfortable with repeated climbs and descents. Although relatively short in distance, the route includes several demanding sections that will test both stamina and tandem handling. Good communication between pilot and stoker will be particularly important throughout the hillier parts of the journey.

  • Distance: 27.3 miles (approximately 44 km), starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club.
  • Climbing: Binn Hill is tackled twice, first southbound and later northbound. The return journey also includes the ascent of Cullaloe Pass, making careful pacing important.
  • Descending: Jawbanes Road eastbound and Redpath Brae provide notable downhill sections, with further descending on the second crossing of Binn Hill. Tandem pilots should manage speed carefully and allow plenty of braking distance.
  • Route character: A varied circuit through coastal towns, inland settlements and hilly countryside, with frequent changes in gradient. Crews should expect more than one substantial effort rather than a single main climb.
  • Tandem considerations: Low gearing will be helpful on the steeper sections. Pilots and stokers should communicate clearly before climbs, descents and changes of pace, particularly when approaching junctions.
  • Refreshments and stamina: A lunch stop is suggested at The Nest Café beside Kinghorn Loch, with alternatives in Burntisland. Allow sufficient time and energy for the second Binn Hill crossing and Cullaloe Pass, both of which come after the suggested lunch stop.
