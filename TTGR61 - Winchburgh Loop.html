Scenery and points of interest

The Winchburgh Loop takes Talking Tandems riders on an enjoyable 23.1-mile journey from Dalgety Bay into West Lothian and back. Starting at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club, the route heads through Inverkeithing before crossing the Forth into the Edinburgh area and continuing towards Dalmeny.

From Dalmeny, the ride continues through Kirkliston and west towards Winchburgh, taking in a mixture of towns, villages and countryside along the way. Winchburgh provides a good opportunity for a refreshment break, with Winchburgh Tearooms on Main Street identified as a possible stop, subject to a recce.

The return journey heads back towards Edinburgh and North Queensferry before crossing the Forth once more and making its way through Inverkeithing to Dalgety Bay Sailing Club. With a variety of places to pass through and some pleasant stretches between them, this is an enjoyable outing for those looking to venture a little further afield.

Terrain and cycling difficulty

The Winchburgh Loop is a moderately difficult, 23.1-mile ride offering a good balance of distance and manageable climbing. It is a worthwhile outing for tandem crews comfortable with a couple of hours or more in the saddle, with additional time for stops and group riding.

  • Distance: Approximately 23.1 miles (37.2 km), starting and finishing at Dalgety Bay Sailing Club.
  • Climbing: The available route profile suggests around 164 metres of cumulative ascent. Climbing is relatively modest overall, although shorter uphill sections may require additional effort from both pilot and stoker.
  • Descents: Approximately 163 metres of cumulative descent are indicated. Tandem pilots should allow extra braking distance and communicate changes in speed before downhill sections.
  • Route character: The ride passes through Inverkeithing, Dalmeny, Kirkliston, Winchburgh and North Queensferry. Pilots should be prepared for navigation through several communities and the associated junctions.
  • Effort and pacing: The moderately difficult classification reflects a ride of reasonable length with some changes in elevation. A steady, comfortable pace and regular communication between pilot and stoker will help everyone enjoy the outing.
  • Tandem considerations: Allow extra room for manoeuvring, particularly at junctions and turns. Clear verbal warnings about stopping, starting, bends and changes in gradient will help blind and partially sighted stoker riders anticipate the journey.
